The useful bit

Why a small, steady SIP can become a meaningful corpus

A Systematic Investment Plan (SIP) lets you invest a fixed amount at regular intervals, usually every month, into a mutual fund. Instead of waiting for the “perfect” market moment, you build a repeatable habit. The calculator above estimates what that habit could be worth when your investment earns a hypothetical annual return.

For the estimate, the monthly rate is derived by dividing the expected annual rate by 12. The future value formula is FV = P × [((1 + r)n − 1) / r] × (1 + r), where P is your monthly investment, r is the monthly rate, and n is the total number of monthly deposits. For example, a ₹10,000 SIP for 10 years at 12% uses 120 deposits and a 1% monthly rate. Your total contribution is ₹12,00,000; the remaining value is the effect of compounding.

SIPs can make investing easier because they automate consistency and spread purchases across market conditions. They are not risk-free: mutual fund returns fluctuate, and an illustration is never a promise. Choose an investment that matches your time horizon and risk comfort, review costs, and increase your SIP as income grows. Keeping an emergency fund separate can also help you avoid selling investments at an inconvenient time.

Practical habits that help

  • Set a goal and time horizon before choosing a fund.
  • Review your plan once or twice a year, not every day.
  • Use the calculator with conservative return assumptions.

Common questions

Does the calculator guarantee my final value?+

No. It is a projection based on a constant assumed return. Actual mutual fund performance can be higher or lower.

What happens if I increase my SIP?+

A higher monthly contribution increases both the amount invested and the potential compounding base.

Should I use the highest return assumption?+

Use a reasonable, conservative range and compare scenarios rather than relying on one optimistic number.

The useful bit

Make the full cost of a loan visible

An Equated Monthly Instalment (EMI) is the regular payment made toward a loan. It combines a portion of principal with interest. At the beginning of most amortising loans, the interest portion is larger because the outstanding balance is highest. As you repay principal, the interest component gradually falls.

The standard EMI formula is E = P × r × (1 + r)n / ((1 + r)n − 1). P is the loan principal, r is the monthly interest rate, and n is the number of monthly payments. For a ₹25,00,000 loan at 8.5% for 20 years, divide 8.5% by 12 for r and use 240 payments. The resulting EMI is roughly ₹21,696, though the lender’s exact figure can vary with rounding, fees, or a changing rate.

Use this tool to compare tenure choices, not just the smallest EMI. Extending the tenure can reduce the monthly payment but often increases total interest substantially. Before borrowing, account for processing charges, insurance, taxes, and prepayment terms. A healthy budget leaves room for essential expenses, savings, and unexpected changes in income.

Before you borrow

  • Compare total repayment, not only the monthly number.
  • Keep a buffer for rate changes and irregular expenses.
  • Ask the lender for a complete amortisation schedule.

Common questions

Why does a longer tenure cost more?+

You make more monthly payments, so interest has more time to accumulate on the outstanding balance.

Does this include lender fees?+

No. Add processing fees, insurance, taxes, and any other charges to understand your full borrowing cost.

Can I reduce total interest?+

A larger down payment, shorter tenure, lower rate, or well-timed prepayments can reduce interest, subject to lender terms.

The useful bit

Know whether tax is inside or outside the price

GST calculations become confusing when a quote does not clearly say whether tax is included. This calculator handles both common situations. Choose “Add tax” when you have a pre-tax amount and want the final customer price. Choose “Remove tax” when a displayed price already includes GST and you need to find the original taxable value.

For an exclusive calculation, GST = base amount × tax rate ÷ 100, and the final price = base amount + GST. On ₹1,00,000 at 18%, the tax is ₹18,000 and the final price is ₹1,18,000. For an inclusive calculation, the base amount = inclusive price ÷ (1 + tax rate ÷ 100). At 18%, a ₹1,18,000 displayed price contains a ₹1,00,000 base and ₹18,000 GST.

For business use, keep invoices and tax records aligned with the applicable rules for your jurisdiction. Tax rates can differ by product, service, location, and registration status. The calculator is useful for estimates and conversations, but your accounting records should follow current official guidance or advice from a qualified professional.

Good pricing hygiene

  • Label every quote as tax-inclusive or tax-exclusive.
  • Show the tax rate and tax amount separately on invoices.
  • Confirm the current rate before finalising a customer price.

Common questions

What is the difference between the two modes?+

Add tax starts with a pre-tax amount. Remove tax starts with a price that already includes tax.

Can rates differ by item?+

Yes. Different goods and services may have different applicable rates or exemptions.

Is this an invoice generator?+

No. It provides a quick estimate and does not create a tax-compliant invoice.

The useful bit

Dates are more useful when the gap is precise

An age calculator gives you an exact calendar answer instead of an approximation based on dividing days by 365.25. That distinction matters because calendar months have different lengths and leap years add an extra day. This tool calculates completed years first, then completed months, followed by the remaining days.

To use it, select a date of birth and the date you want to measure against. For example, someone born on 15 March 2000 is 26 years old on 15 March 2026. On 27 May 2026, the same person is 26 years, 2 months, and 12 days old. The total-days figure is a separate useful measure for contracts, milestones, and simple date comparisons.

For a reliable result, check that the birth date is not in the future and that your “as of” date reflects the relevant local date. In legal, medical, employment, or eligibility contexts, rules can define age and deadlines differently. Treat this as a general-purpose planning tool and confirm any formal requirement with the organisation setting it.

Useful date habits

  • Use the date shown on the official document for formal work.
  • Check time zones when comparing dates from different locations.
  • For deadlines, confirm whether the start or end date counts.

Common questions

Why is calendar age different from total days?+

Calendar age respects month lengths and leap years; total days is simply the elapsed day count.

Can I calculate a future age?+

Yes. Set the “as of” date in the future to see the age on that date.

Does the tool account for leap years?+

Yes. It uses the browser’s calendar date behavior when calculating years, months, days, and total days.
